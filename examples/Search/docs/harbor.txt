Harbor notes

The lamp at the end of the pier was out again before dawn. Mara carried
a spare lantern down the slick boards and hung it where the channel
bends. The glass was warm from the last watch.

By midmorning the fog had lifted off the harbor and the pilings showed
their tide line. A skiff came in with two crates of nails and a letter
for the chandlery. Nobody on the skiff had seen the buoy drift.

The lantern is the mark the night boats use. When it fails, they hold
outside the bar and wait for a light they trust. Mara wrote that down
in the margin of the tide book, next to a sketch of the bend.

In the afternoon the wind backed and the water in the slip went opaque.
She walked the length of the pier twice, checking cleats, and found the
lantern swinging on a frayed hook. The hook was new last spring. Salt
had already bitten the threads.

After dark she lit the lantern herself and stayed until the first boat
answered with a dip of its own lamp. The channel was a dark road with
one bright stitch at the bend. She could hear the bell buoy and, under
it, the small talk of water against the pilings.

The next entry in the tide book is only a time and the word lantern,
underlined, so the morning watch would know the light had been tended.
