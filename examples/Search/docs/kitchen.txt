Kitchen card

Brown the onions before the garlic goes in, or the garlic burns and the
sauce tastes sharp. Salt the pot, then simmer until the spoon leaves a
track. A café cook taught me to taste at the simmer, not after the heat
is gone.

The lamp over the board is too warm and makes the dough look done early.
Move the board to the side table. The window there is north light.

For the beans: cover with water, bring them up, pour that water off, then
cover again and simmer. Salt late. A full page of text in an old manual
says to soak overnight. The pot on this stove does not care. It wants
an hour at a quiet boil and a lid that does not quite seal.

Bread wants less yeast than the packet suggests and more time than the
card on the fridge admits. Score the top. Bake until the base sounds
hollow. Set it on a rack, not on the plate, or the bottom sweats.

If the sauce splits, take it off the heat and whisk in a spoon of cold
water. Do not salt it again until it comes back together.
